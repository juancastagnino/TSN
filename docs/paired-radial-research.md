# Paired-radial and shared-motion investigation

## Lunar-plane experiment

The accepted `paired-radial` baseline uses two equal vectors in world XZ, constructed from the uncorrected geocentric Moon direction and apsidal axis. The experiment transforms the uncorrected Earth–Moon vector into the rotating apsidal parent frame, builds the same pair in its XZ plane, and transforms the result back to world space. The parent inherits the tilted, precessing lunar plane. Solar coupling is unchanged and both pairs are sampled before either displacement is applied, without feedback. This changes the model position, not merely export numbers.

`lunarPlaneBlend` is 0 for the pushed world-XZ baseline and 1 for the lunar-plane experiment; intermediate values linearly blend the resulting displacements. Radius remains -0.007055 per vector. The new default is 1 for the test. Old settings imported through the merge process can inherit the current value, so explicitly select 0 when reproducing the original method. Compare 0 and 1 with identical dates, settings and physical-size mode. Neither mode proves a physical breathing mechanism; the local projected Moon angle can itself be nonuniform, so uniform rotation of each vector is not established. Solar coupling may also displace the resulting position out of the lunar plane. The geometry is a translation dependent on the instantaneous uncorrected Moon position, not proof that an independent rigid orbit physically breathes.

## Cross-body and whirlpool hypotheses

The current research reports available at investigation time have only Moon and Sun residuals for 1800-06-21 through 2026-06-21 (daily). The Moon RA FFT lists about 365.25 d, 205.85 d and 13.78 d; the Sun RA FFT lists about 363.64 d and 182.62 d. These are finite-window peaks in separate geocentric RA residuals, not evidence of a common force or coherent phase. An annual feature may also arise from reference-frame, apparent-versus-geometric, projection, phase or parameter differences. Mars, Mercury and Venus matched residuals are not present in these reports, so no empirical claim about a shared pattern is justified yet.

For a testable vortex-like *kinematic* idea, define a named center, axis, phase driver and a vector displacement law. Compare (a) no coupling, (b) a Sun–Mars-relative phase, and (c) a shared Earth-observer displacement with its predicted body-dependent parallax, using only hierarchy-derived phases rather than independently fitted periods. A rotating pattern alone does not establish a fluid, magnetic or gravitational whirlpool. A common translation of observer and every target cancels in geocentric vectors; an observer-only shift affects bodies according to distance and direction and must not be confused with an Earth-hosted Moon shift. Sun-hosted motions may transmit a perturbation to multiple bodies, but that is not equivalent to a global observer displacement.

Export matched Moon, Sun, Mars, Venus and Mercury ephemerides for the same interval and time grid and compare against matching JPL products. Use 1800–2026 daily for historical stability plus a dense 2000–2026 interval to check short-period peaks and aliasing. Analyze signed tangent-plane angular residuals and distances; compare residuals against model-relative Sun–Mars, Sun–planet and Earth–planet phases, including phase and direction, not FFT amplitude alone. Check RA wrapping, ecliptic/equatorial projections, light-time/apparent frame, differing distance definitions, sample window and aliased periods. Validate hypotheses on held-out dates; identify close periods and shared drivers before adding free parameters. Compare any candidate with controls that preserve the already good Sun and Moon, and quantify changes in every body and distance.
